NULL-VERSUS-REAL MICROBIOME DA CONSOLIDATION
====================================================================

The canonical null simulation contains zero true DA taxa.
Therefore, any discovery in the null dataset is a false discovery for that realization.

The real microbiome datasets do not have known biological ground truth.
Real-data significant taxa and consensus signals must therefore be interpreted as statistical discoveries and cross-method agreement, not proven true positives.

200-RUN NULL CALIBRATION
------------------------
CLR/Wilcoxon: 3.5% of runs contained at least one false discovery.
ALDEx2: 3.0% of runs contained at least one false discovery.
ANCOM-BC: 9.5% of runs contained at least one false discovery.
DESeq2: 14.0% of runs contained at least one false discovery.

CONSENSUS NULL CALIBRATION
--------------------------
2-of-4 consensus: 7.0% probability of at least one false consensus discovery.
3-of-4 consensus: 3.0% probability of at least one false consensus discovery.
4-of-4 consensus: 2.0% probability of at least one false consensus discovery.

COMPARABILITY NOTES
-------------------
Discovery rates are normalized by each dataset's analysis universe because raw significant-taxon counts are not directly comparable.
DESeq2 valid-q discovery rates are retained because some datasets contain many NA adjusted p-values.
Standard ANCOM calls are used for cross-dataset comparison. Robust ANCOM calls are retained separately where available.
Peggy's ALDEx2 analysis is Welch-primary, whereas the other ALDEx2 workflows are Wilcoxon-primary.
Consensus definitions differ somewhat among team workflows and are explicitly labeled.

MAIN INTERPRETATION
-------------------
Discovery burden varies strongly across both methods and real datasets.
The magnitude of this variation is substantially greater than the average false-positive burden observed under repeated complete-null simulation.
However, because biological truth is unknown in the real datasets, larger discovery sets cannot automatically be interpreted as greater accuracy.
The results instead demonstrate strong method-by-dataset dependence.
Consensus provides a way to prioritize more reproducible signals, but the null results show that consensus itself is not equivalent to truth.
